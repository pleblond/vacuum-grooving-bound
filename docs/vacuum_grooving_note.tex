\title{First search for cumulative vacuum index creep (`vacuum grooving') in cryogenic silicon cavity drift records}
\author{Philippe Leblond}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Long-term drift records of cryogenic silicon cavities can bound cumulative vacuum refractive-index creep of the form $\Delta n_{\rm vac}=\alpha_E\!\int\! P_{\rm circ}\,dt$, a signature with no counterpart in instantaneous vacuum-nonlinearity searches. No prior study has tested or bounded vacuum hysteresis; we fill that observational gap using published open data. Clock frequency ratios erase cavity drift by construction; the servo's AOM correction is the raw drift observable. We present an open-data analysis that regresses per-epoch drift rates against circulating power together with thermal and silicon-aging terms, validated on synthetic logs with known injected coefficients. Applied to the published decade-long drift histories of Si2, Si3, Si5 (124K) and Si6 (17K), digitized settled rates are Si2 $-57\pm8$, Si3 $-20\pm4$, Si5 $-18\pm13$, Si6 $+18\pm13$ $\mu$Hz/s, with the Si2 record integrating to $-29.5$ kHz over days 260--3798 (published $-44$ kHz/10yr; the gap implies $-646\,\mu$Hz/s mean over the steep un-digitized days 0--260, consistent with Si3's $-883$ at day 269). With no published power logs, we report conditional bounds: had the circulating power varied by $\ge X$ across epochs with no correlated drift beyond the settled scatter $\sigma$, then $|\alpha|\le\sigma/\Delta P$. For Si6 ($\sigma=12.5\,\mu$Hz/s, $P_{\rm circ}\approx13.5$ mW, the only cavity with published power) this gives $|\Delta n|<1.5\times10^{-13}$/yr per 100\,$\mu$W under plausible $\ge10\%$ excursions ($1.5\times10^{-12}$ at 1\%, $1.5\times10^{-14}$ at factor 2). The leading systematic is the known power-dependent drift of cryogenic cavities; reaching $10^{-19}$ requires logged power and temperature or an intentional power dither.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Existing searches for optical vacuum nonlinearities---PVLAS \cite{pvlas2020}, BMV \cite{battesti2013}, OVAL \cite{oval2017}---focus exclusively on instantaneous responses ($n_2 I$) induced by strong fields, assuming a vacuum state with zero memory. Similarly, tests of Lorentz violation or time-variation of fundamental constants using optical cavities treat long-term cavity drift as a pure background systematic and actively subtract it \cite{muller2003,kennedy2020}. To our knowledge, no prior study has sought to test or constrain cumulative vacuum plasticity, i.e.\ a persistent optical-path change proportional to integrated photon dose, $\Delta n_{\rm vac}\propto\int P_{\rm circ}\,dt$. The Euler--Heisenberg effective action \cite{heisenberg1936} predicts an instantaneous vacuum Kerr index $n_2\approx1.6\times10^{-33}$ cm$^2$/W \cite{vackerr2019} with no cumulative term; PVLAS bounds static-field vacuum birefringence at $\Delta n=(12\pm17)\times10^{-23}$ at 2.5 T \cite{pvlas2020}. Axion-like particles add instantaneous EHS-like terms \cite{alpehs2018} and dispersive corrections \cite{vackerr2019}, while domain walls imprint discrete jumps rather than dose accumulation. While Standard Model QED predicts no hysteresis, we adopt the cumulative relation as a purely phenomenological parametrization to establish the first quantitative upper bounds on vacuum memory from multi-year cryogenic cavity drift records.

Cryogenic silicon cavities in cryogenic UHV, operated at 17--124K in this analysis, drift at $\sim10^{-19}$/s fractional \cite{lee2026} --- roughly $10^3\times$ slower than typical ULE cavities (kHz/day vs Hz/day \cite{lee2026}), enabling a DC search for dose-dependent effects.

\section{Method}
Clock ratios ($\nu_{Yb+}/\nu_{Sr}$) are atoms-vs-atoms, servoed to $10^{-18}$; cavity drift is erased from them by construction. The servo's AOM correction $\nu_{corr}(t)$ is atoms-minus-cavity: the raw DC ruler for cavity length plus vacuum index. With atoms absolute and cavity $\nu=mc/2nL$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\nu_{corr}(t) = -\nu_0\left(\frac{\Delta L_{Si}}{L}+\Delta n_{vac}\right).
\end{equation}
We fit drift rates $s_i=d\nu_{corr}/dt$ per epoch $i$ (10-day bins matching published drift records):
\begin{equation}
s_i = \beta_{Si}(t) + \alpha \langle P_{\rm circ}\rangle_i + \gamma_1\Delta T_i + \gamma_2\Delta T_i^2 + \epsilon_i,
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{Si}$ is silicon aging, $\alpha$ the target grooving coefficient in Hz/s per Watt, $\gamma_{1,2}$ the thermal coupling (quadratic term retained for the 124K zero-crossing, where $d\alpha/dT\sim10^{-11}$/K$^2$ makes 0.1 mK $\to$ $10^{-15}$), and $\epsilon_i$ residual noise. With constant power, $\beta$ and $\alpha\langle P\rangle$ are degenerate; power variation across epochs breaks the degeneracy.

Three details matter. First, temperature enters as regressors, never pre-subtracted, preserving covariance; even at zero-crossings, sub-mK fluctuations induce $10^{-15}$--$10^{-16}$ shifts. Second, AOM logs are frequency offsets in Hz, not wrapped phase: slopes are fit on the raw offset within each locked epoch (unlock and mode-jump steps handled by epoch grouping), with nominal AOM frequency subtracted first; phase unwrapping applies only to raw beat-note phase. Cavity frequency is tracked against masers calibrated to timescales (e.g.\ UTC(NIST) \cite{robinson2019}) or against optical clocks with standard systematic corrections. Third, $P_{\rm circ}=P_{trans}\mathcal{F}/\pi$ uses time-interpolated ring-down finesse $\mathcal{F}(t)$ when available (Si2/Si3 finesse $\approx$ 500\,000 \cite{matei2017}, Si6 470\,000 \cite{lee2026}); else $P_{trans}$ itself serves as proxy regressor, conservative against finesse degradation over the 5--10 yr span. Epoch slopes use Allan weights $w_i=1/\sigma_{{\rm Allan},i}^2$ where available.

Silicon aging is visibly time-dependent (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}), so $\beta_{Si}(t)$ is treated two independent ways: (a) separate intercepts per time window, and (b) a joint $\beta_0+\beta_1\exp(-t/\tau)$ fit with $\tau$ found by grid search. Both give consistent answers (Sec.~\ref{sec:sys}); the headline numbers use settled windows where aging is approximately constant.

The cumulative (fatigue) form fits $\Delta\nu_{corr}(t)=\beta_{Si}t+\alpha_E E_{cum}(t)+\gamma\!\int\!\Delta T\,dt'+\sum C_i{\bf1}_i+\epsilon$ with $E_{cum}(t)=\int P_{circ}I_{on}dt$ never reset (flatlining when unlocked); step dummies $C_i$ absorb relock offsets. The pipeline ingests $\nu_{corr}$, $P_{trans}$, $T$, and lock flags, keeps thermal terms as regressors, runs the epoch regression plus the cumulative cross-check, and reports the $H_0$: $\alpha=0$ bound.

\section{Open data}
\label{sec:open}
Lee et al.\ report drift rates of four silicon cavities versus age in their Fig.~3b, computed from 10-day binned frequencies \cite{lee2026}. The arXiv source package contains the figure images but no machine-readable supplement, so we digitized Fig.~3b by color segmentation against hand-calibrated broken-log axes, taking median rates in 55 log-spaced age bins per cavity. Per-point digitization error is $\sim$15\%, dominated by axis calibration; x-axis tick labels intruding into the plot area were blanked. The digitization reproduces the published recent rates and the Si2 cumulative integral (Fig.~\ref{fig:digitized}, Table~\ref{tab:cav}).

Using $\Delta\nu/\nu=-\Delta L/L=-\Delta n$ with $L_{\rm vac}\sim L$ at $\nu=194$ THz, the lead bound is $1.5\times10^{-13}$/yr per 100$\mu$W. Models requiring $\gtrsim10^{-12}$/yr are excluded only if $\ge1\%$ excursions can be established. The $10^{-19}$ target is $\sim$6 orders below the 10\% projection and requires measured variation.

\section{Systematics and discussion}
\label{sec:sys}
The leading systematic is power-dependent drift itself: Robinson et al.\ (Si4, 4K) found drift linear in incident power, lowest $-3\times10^{-19}$/s at 40 nW transmitted and extrapolating to zero at zero power, with DC intensity coupling $1\times10^{-12}$/$\mu$W and power stabilized to pW \cite{robinson2019}. Any grooving signal must be disentangled from this effect; its mechanism is the key open question. The DC photothermal numbers that matter are offsets $\propto P$, distinct from a drift rate $\propto P$: Matei's $1.7\times10^{-15}$/$\mu$W transmitted at 124K \cite{matei2017} and Robinson's $1\times10^{-12}$/$\mu$W at 4K \cite{robinson2019}. Dynamic photothermal response was characterized on GW-like dielectric coatings at 1064 nm \cite{farsi2011}, not on these mirrors; dither waveforms separate offset from slope. Power-dependent coating systematics (birefringent and photoinduced noise in crystalline mirrors) are further documented \cite{kedar2023,yu2023}.

Aging is controlled, not just caveated: for Si2 the windowed treatment (split day 2000: early $-112\pm88$, late $-59\pm12\,\mu$Hz/s) and the joint exponential ($\tau\approx1.8$ yr, $\beta_0=-52\,\mu$Hz/s, residuals $11\,\mu$Hz/s over days $\ge800$) agree, while 1/t gives an unphysical $-4$ asymptote and is disfavored (Fig.~\ref{fig:aging}). The settled-window aging slope is $+2\,\mu$Hz/s/yr.

\caption{Si2 aging treatments compared: window medians (split day 2000, dashed) and joint exponential ($\tau\approx1.8$ yr, solid) vs 1/t (dotted) over days $\ge800$. Residuals $\approx$11--12 $\mu$Hz/s under both viable treatments.}

Maser reference drift is common-mode and $\sim$$10\times$ below cavity drift \cite{lee2026}. Long-term 4K drift remains unreported (Zhang et al.\ bounded $<1$ mHz/s over 3 days \cite{zhang2017}), so the cleanest $\beta\approx0$ regime is still to be exploited. A differential Si2--Si3 analysis would cancel common-mode terms; Matei et al.\ report $100\,\mu$Hz/s relative drift and run with no active intensity stabilization \cite{matei2017}, so the auxiliary logs may already contain usable power variation.

\section{Outlook}
Requesting 10-day means of $P_{\rm trans}$, $T_{\rm cryo}$, and $\mathcal{F}$ (2015--2026) from PTB and JILA costs nothing and unlocks the full regression; even a single 10\% power step during commissioning reaches $\sim$$10^{-13}$. An intentional Si6 dither, alternating 13.5/27 mW monthly with synchronous demodulation (common drift cancels), reaches $\sigma_\alpha=\sqrt{2}\sigma_m/(\Delta P\sqrt{K})$ over $K$ month-pairs, i.e.\ $\Delta n/{\rm yr}\approx1.7\times10^{-15}(\sigma_m/1\,\mu{\rm Hz/s})/\sqrt{K}$ per 100$\mu$W: $1.7\times10^{-15}$ in 2 months ($K=1$), $7.0\times10^{-16}$ in one alternating year ($K=6$) at optimistic $\sigma_m=1\,\mu$Hz/s ($5.1\times10^{-15}$ / $2.1\times10^{-15}$ at realistic $\sigma_m=3\,\mu$Hz/s; Allan floor $\sim2\times10^{-11}$ Hz/s negligible). Cross-lab comparison of $\alpha_{PTB}$ vs $\alpha_{JILA}$ at matched vacuum would test common-mode origin.
